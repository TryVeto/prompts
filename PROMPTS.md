# Prompt library

Each prompt uses the current conversation by default. Notes are optional: add only what changes or needs emphasis.

## 1 · Think it through

Objective:

- Resolve the question with evidence, clear reasoning, and a useful conclusion.

Key results:

- The actual question is answered directly.
- Facts, assumptions, and uncertainties are distinguished.
- The conclusion follows from the evidence and reasoning.

Milestones:

1. The question and relevant prior decisions are established.
2. The evidence needed to answer it is recovered or gathered.
3. Credible explanations or alternatives are compared.
4. The leading conclusion is checked against contrary evidence.
5. The answer and its practical implications are delivered.

Instructions:

Use the active question or decision in this conversation. Notes below add or change the brief; blank Notes means use the existing context. Recover only missing, relevant information. Do not ask me to repeat available context or restart settled work. If no subject can be identified, ask one focused question.

Separate the outcome I want from my first suggested solution. Reason from first principles and verify pivotal facts against original sources, using current evidence where needed. For consequential choices, compare objectives, alternatives, consequences, and trade-offs. Do not use “we need more data” to avoid reasoning you can do now; obtain accessible evidence when it would change the answer.

Challenge your preferred conclusion. Explain the strongest objection, the uncertainty that matters, and what would change your answer. Use depth where it improves the conclusion, not to fill a page or time quota. Credit milestones already satisfied; they are not mandatory new research rounds.

Lead with the answer or recommendation, then the decisive reasoning, sources, trade-offs, and remaining uncertainty. Include a practical next action when relevant. Complete the analysis, but do not turn an analytical request into an unrequested implementation or change live systems.

## 2 · Develop the idea

Objective:

- Turn the promising idea into a concrete candidate worth pursuing.

Key results:

- An inspectable candidate exists in the intended form.
- It improves the main weakness without losing accepted strengths.
- A realistic test establishes its strengths and remaining limits.

Milestones:

1. The starting point and accepted strengths are established.
2. The weakness most limiting the idea is identified.
3. Meaningfully different solutions are compared.
4. The strongest approach becomes a coherent candidate.
5. The candidate is tested and compared with the previous best.
6. The strongest version and unresolved production work are delivered.

Instructions:

Use the idea or selected direction currently being discussed. Notes below add or change the brief; blank Notes means continue from the existing context. Recover only missing, relevant work and feedback. Do not ask me to repeat available context. If no idea can be identified, ask one focused question.

Treat the idea as worth developing, not as fully solved. If the direction is unsettled, compare genuinely different approaches and recommend one. If it is selected, explore solutions within it rather than reopening the whole project. Use excellent, applicable references to improve the mechanism, structure, or experience—not merely its appearance.

Make the smallest serious realization that lets me judge the whole idea in its intended medium: a passage, connected interaction, storyboard, or working technical path. Do not substitute a plan, polished fragment, or description for the candidate. Development does not imply a full production build unless requested.

Test the important assumption and a difficult, realistic case. Inspect the actual result, compare it with the previous best in the same context, and preserve the stronger version. If repeated edits do not improve the weakness, change the method. If the direction itself fails, explain the evidence and recommend a focused change rather than silently replacing it.

Reuse completed milestones. Continue while important, solvable gaps remain within scope and budget. Lead with the developed work, then what improved, what the checks establish, and what remains unresolved. Preserve earlier versions and existing permissions; do not claim testing or approval that did not occur.

## 3 · Do the work

Objective:

- Finish the current assignment and deliver a result I can actually use.

Key results:

- The requested work is complete, not merely described.
- The delivered version passes the relevant checks.
- The result is accessible in the agreed destination.

Milestones:

1. The current source and completion requirements are established.
2. The required work and applicable feedback are implemented.
3. The exact result is inspected in its intended use.
4. Material defects are repaired and affected checks rerun.
5. The finished result is delivered and its final state verified.

Instructions:

Use the active assignment and latest applicable feedback in this conversation. Notes below add or change the brief; blank Notes means proceed from the current state. Recover only missing, relevant context and identify the actual source of record. Do not ask me to repeat available information. If no assignment can be identified, ask one focused question.

Establish what must change, what must remain, and what completion means. Then do the work—not just a plan, brief, or description of changes. Use the research, references, tools, and skills needed. Resolve ordinary details yourself. Reuse completed milestones rather than treating them as separate tasks requiring another prompt.

When revising, apply feedback to the current version and all affected parts. Check that another reviewer’s recommendations are valid before adopting them. Preserve accepted strengths and keep the previous best recoverable. Do not turn a finishing pass into a redesign or confuse shorter work with better work.

Inspect and test the actual result in the form I will use. Repair material defects and rerun the relevant checks. Compare revisions with the previous best; change the method if repeated patches do not help. Continue while consequential requirements remain unresolved and useful work is possible within scope. Do not invent extra work to fill a quota.

Deliver the result in the requested format and authorized destination, with editable source where applicable. Verify the saved or resulting state. Lead with the deliverable, then what changed, what you checked, and any remaining gap with its smallest unblock. Respect existing ownership, permissions, and budgets; approval covers only the specified action and payload. Never invent execution or completion.

## 4 · Review the work

Objective:

- Judge whether the current work is ready, without changing it.

Key results:

- The verdict matches what was actually inspected.
- Material findings have evidence and concrete corrections.
- Strengths, judgment calls, and untested risks are explicit.

Milestones:

1. The exact candidate, brief, and quality bar are identified.
2. The actual artifact and important user paths are inspected.
3. Consequential findings are verified and ranked.
4. Strengths and inspection limits are recorded.
5. The verdict and necessary corrections are delivered.

Instructions:

Review the current candidate or work being discussed in this conversation. Notes below add or change the review focus; blank Notes means use the existing brief and feedback. Recover only missing, relevant context. Do not ask me to repeat available information. If no target can be identified, ask one focused question.

Inspect the actual work, not just its description or the maker’s completion report. Assess functional correctness, clarity, craft, and readiness for the intended stage. Compare against the accepted brief and governing reference. Do not demand production machinery from a concept study or reopen settled decisions without material evidence.

Look for consequential omissions, contradictions, regressions, and unsupported claims. Independently assess other reviews rather than inheriting their conclusions. Do not invent criticism to appear rigorous. Name the strengths that must survive any revision.

Use authorized, isolated checks when interaction is necessary. Do not implement fixes, alter live data, or publish anything. Reuse still-valid evidence, but do not treat checks of an older version as proof of the current one. State exactly what could not be inspected or tested.

Lead with “Ready,” “Revise,” or “Insufficient evidence.” Rank findings by consequence, explaining the evidence, effect on the user, and concrete correction. Separate observed defects from preferences and untested risks. End with only the decisions or corrections that matter. Do not imply that recommendations are implemented or that your verdict is my approval.
